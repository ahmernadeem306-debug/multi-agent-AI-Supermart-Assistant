# Shrinkage and Loss Prevention SOP

**Document type:** SOP · **Owner:** Loss Prevention Lead · **Review cycle:** quarterly

## Purpose

This procedure defines how inventory loss is categorised, recorded, valued,
investigated and written off. It is the reference the operations assistant
uses when it reports a shrinkage spike or a shelf/backroom discrepancy.

## Shrinkage categories

Every unit of loss is recorded against exactly one reason code:

- **damage** — breakage, crushing, spillage, cold-chain failure, or an item
  returned in unsaleable condition.
- **theft** — external theft, internal theft, or stock consumed on the
  premises without payment.
- **expiry** — perishable stock reaching its expiry or use-by date unsold and
  pulled from the shelf.
- **admin_error** — receiving errors, miscounts, mis-scans at the register,
  price-file errors, and unrecorded transfers.

Accurate coding matters: the four codes drive different corrective actions and
different escalation paths.

## Recording and valuation

- Loss is recorded the same shift it is found, with SKU, quantity, reason code
  and a short note.
- Loss is valued at **unit cost**, not retail price, for shrinkage reporting.
  Retail impact is estimated separately for lost-margin analysis.
- Discrepancies found during a cycle count are recorded as loss (or as a
  positive adjustment) only after a recount confirms them.

## Thresholds requiring investigation

A shrinkage **event** is a single recorded loss. A shrinkage **spike** is what
triggers investigation:

- Any single **theft** event of 10 units or more.
- A SKU accumulating **more than 30 units of loss in any reason code within a
  rolling 30-day window**, when its prior 30-day baseline was materially lower.
- Total shrinkage value on one aisle exceeding 1.5% of that aisle's cost of
  goods sold for the period.
- Any shelf/backroom discrepancy that a recount cannot explain.

When a threshold is crossed, Loss Prevention opens an investigation: pull the
sales and receiving history for the SKU, review CCTV for the affected window,
check for admin_error causes first (they are the most common and the easiest
to fix), and only then treat the loss as theft.

## Write-off approval

| Write-off value (at cost) | Approver |
|---|---|
| Up to 50.00 | Shift Lead |
| 50.01 – 250.00 | Store Manager |
| 250.01 – 1,000.00 | Store Manager + Loss Prevention Lead |
| Over 1,000.00 | Regional Operations |

Expiry write-offs above the Store Manager limit also require a note explaining
why demand was over-forecast or the batch over-ordered, so purchasing can
adjust. A large perishable batch expiring at once is both an expiry write-off
and a likely cause of a subsequent stockout — record both.

## Corrective actions by cause

- **theft:** physical measures (relocation to a secured bay, tagging, staffing
  the aisle at peak), plus an incident report.
- **expiry:** review the reorder point and order quantity; tighten FEFO
  discipline; start markdowns earlier (at two days to expiry, not one).
- **damage:** inspect handling and storage; check the cold chain; raise a
  supplier claim if the stock arrived compromised.
- **admin_error:** retrain on receiving and scanning; correct the price file;
  reconcile transfers.

## Reporting

Shrinkage is reviewed weekly by aisle and by reason code. A rising theft trend
on one aisle, or a repeated unexplained discrepancy on one SKU, is reported to
the Store Manager with the supporting data attached.
