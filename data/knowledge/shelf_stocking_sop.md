# Shelf Stocking Standard Operating Procedure

**Document type:** SOP · **Owner:** Store Operations Manager · **Review cycle:** quarterly

## Purpose and scope

This procedure governs how shelf and backroom stock is replenished across all
eight sales aisles — Produce, Dairy, Bakery, Frozen, Beverages, Snacks,
Household and Personal Care. It applies to every associate who touches
saleable stock and is the reference used when the operations assistant reports
a stocking or facing problem.

## Facing and presentation

Every SKU must be fronted and faced at the start of each shift and again after
the lunch peak. A "face" means the front row of product is pulled forward to
the shelf edge with labels square to the aisle. Gaps wider than one facing must
be closed within 30 minutes, either by pulling backroom stock or by installing
a temporary "temporarily unavailable" shelf tag if no stock exists.

Planogram compliance is mandatory. SKUs are merchandised only in their assigned
bay and shelf position. Associates may not relocate a SKU or expand its facings
without written approval from the category manager, because the demand model
and the reorder points assume the planogram is stable.

## Replenishment thresholds

Each SKU carries two control numbers held in the item master:

- **Reorder point** — approximately five days of average daily demand. When
  on-hand stock (shelf plus backroom) falls to or below the reorder point, the
  SKU is added to the replenishment worklist for the next delivery.
- **Safety stock** — approximately two days of average daily demand. On-hand
  stock should never be allowed to fall below safety stock during normal
  trading. If it does, the shift lead raises a stockout-risk note.

High-velocity SKUs on Beverages and Snacks end-caps are checked twice per
shift because they can move a full facing between checks. Slow movers on
Household and Personal Care are checked once per day.

## Rotation: FIFO and FEFO

All stock is rotated. Non-perishable aisles (Beverages, Snacks, Household,
Personal Care) use **FIFO** — first in, first out — so older delivery batches
sell before newer ones.

Perishable aisles use **FEFO** — first expired, first out. New deliveries are
dated on receipt and placed **behind** existing stock only when their expiry
date is later. If a new batch expires sooner than shelf stock, it goes in
front. Approximate shelf lives used for planning are: Produce 7 days, Bakery
5 days, Dairy 14 days, Frozen 180 days. Any unit within two days of its expiry
date is pulled to the markdown process described in the Shrinkage and Loss
Prevention SOP.

## Backroom discipline

Backroom stock is stored by aisle and then by SKU. Pallet and case quantities
are logged into the stockroom system on receipt so that shelf-plus-backroom
on-hand is always accurate. A recurring mismatch between the recorded on-hand
figure and a physical count is a discrepancy event and must be escalated under
the Inventory Count and Discrepancy SOP.

## Delivery day workflow

1. Confirm the purchase order against the delivery note; record short or
   damaged cases before the driver leaves.
2. Date-stamp every perishable case.
3. Break down and stage stock by aisle.
4. Work the replenishment worklist in this order: stockouts first, then
   below-safety-stock SKUs, then below-reorder-point SKUs.
5. Rotate to FEFO/FIFO as stock goes up.
6. Update the stockroom system so backroom quantities are current.

## Escalation

A SKU that stocks out despite an on-time delivery, or that repeatedly falls
below safety stock, is reported to the Inventory and Supply Chain Manager with
the delivery history attached. Persistent facing gaps on a high-velocity SKU
are treated as a lost-sales issue, not a housekeeping issue.
